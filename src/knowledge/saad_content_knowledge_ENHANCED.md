## SECTION 1: TECHNICAL WRITING FOUNDATIONS

### 1.1 Core Principles of Technical Writing
- **Clarity**: Use precise, unambiguous language that leaves no room for interpretation
- **Conciseness**: Eliminate wordiness without sacrificing completeness or accuracy
- **Accuracy**: Verify all technical details, procedures, and data points
- **Consistency**: Maintain uniform terminology, style, structure, and formatting
- **Completeness**: Cover all necessary information for successful task execution
- **Accessibility**: Write for diverse audiences, abilities, and technical levels
- **Actionability**: Ensure readers can perform tasks directly from documentation
- **Usability**: Design documents for easy navigation, scanning, and reference

### 1.2 The Seven C's of Technical Writing
1. **Clear**: Avoid jargon when possible; define technical terms when necessary
2. **Concise**: Use active voice; eliminate redundancy and filler words
3. **Concrete**: Provide specific examples, data, and measurable outcomes
4. **Correct**: Verify technical accuracy, grammar, spelling, and punctuation
5. **Coherent**: Maintain logical flow, smooth transitions, and clear connections
6. **Complete**: Include all necessary information for task completion
7. **Courteous**: Respect reader's time, intelligence, and expertise level

### 1.3 Writing for Scanability
- Use descriptive headings with clear hierarchy (H1-H6)
- Break content into short paragraphs (3-5 sentences maximum)
- Employ bullet points for lists and numbered lists for sequences
- Highlight key terms using **bold**, *italic*, or `code formatting`
- Use tables for comparative information and structured data
- Include visual breaks (horizontal rules, white space, section dividers)
- Front-load important information (inverted pyramid structure)
- Use parallel structure in lists and headings

### 1.4 Active Voice vs Passive Voice

**Preferred (Active Voice)**:
- "The administrator configures the firewall settings."
- "Click the Submit button to save your changes."
- "The system generates a unique ticket ID automatically."
- "Users report incidents through the service portal."
- "The resolver group investigates the root cause."

**Avoid (Passive Voice)**:
- "The firewall settings are configured by the administrator."
- "The Submit button should be clicked to save changes."
- "A unique ticket ID is automatically generated by the system."
- "Incidents are reported by users through the service portal."
- "The root cause is investigated by the resolver group."

**Exceptions - Passive Voice Acceptable When**:
- The actor is unknown or irrelevant to the process
- Emphasizing the action or result over who performs it
- Maintaining consistency in procedure lists
- Diplomatic phrasing is required ("The request was denied")
- Scientific or technical precision requires passive construction

### 1.5 Readability Formulas and Metrics

**Flesch Reading Ease Score**:
- Formula: 206.835 - 1.015(total words/total sentences) - 84.6(total syllables/total words)
- Target for IT documentation: 60-70 (Plain English)
- 90-100: Very Easy (5th grade)
- 60-70: Standard (8th-9th grade)
- 30-50: Difficult (College level)
- 0-30: Very Difficult (College graduate)

**Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level**:
- Formula: 0.39(total words/total sentences) + 11.8(total syllables/total words) - 15.59
- Target for IT documentation: Grade 8-10
- Service Desk procedures: Grade 6-8
- Technical documentation: Grade 10-12
- Executive summaries: Grade 8-10

**Gunning Fog Index**:
- Formula: 0.4[(words/sentences) + 100(complex words/words)]
- Target: 12 or below for general IT documentation

**Average Sentence Length**:
- Target: 15-20 words per sentence
- Procedures: 10-15 words per sentence
- Conceptual content: 15-25 words per sentence
- Never exceed 30 words in a single sentence

### 1.6 Tone and Voice Guidelines

**Professional Technical Tone**:
- Authoritative but not condescending
- Helpful but not overly casual
- Direct but not abrupt
- Formal but not stuffy
- Confident but not arrogant

**Positive Language**:
- ✅ "Ensure you save your work before closing"
- ❌ "Don't forget to save or you'll lose your work"
- ✅ "Configure the system to enable automatic backups"
- ❌ "The system won't back up unless you configure it"

**Inclusive Language**:
- Use "they/their" as singular pronouns
- Avoid gendered assumptions ("the user... he or she")
- Use role-based terms instead of gendered titles
- Consider cultural and regional variations
- Avoid idioms that don't translate well

### 1.7 Style Guide References

**Microsoft Manual of Style for Technical Communications**:
- Use simple words and short sentences
- Write in second person for procedures ("you")
- Use present tense for current actions
- Avoid jargon; explain technical terms when necessary
- Use parallel structure in lists
- Gender-neutral language throughout

**Chicago Manual of Style (17th Edition)**:
- For formal documentation, white papers, research
- More formal tone than Microsoft style
- Third person acceptable in academic contexts
- Footnotes and citations for references
- Serial comma required in lists

**Google Developer Documentation Style Guide**:
- Conversational tone while remaining professional
- Address the reader directly
- Use present tense
- Active voice for procedures
- Short paragraphs and sentences
- Examples before explanations

**Apple Style Guide**:
- Simple, direct language
- Minimal punctuation
- Avoid technical jargon
- User-focused perspective
- Consistent terminology

## SECTION 2: SOP WRITING MASTERY

### 2.1 SOP Definition and Purpose

A Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) is a detailed, written instruction document that describes the step-by-step process for completing a specific task, operation, or routine activity within an organization. SOPs ensure consistency, quality, safety, and compliance across all operations.

**Primary Purposes**:
- **Consistency**: Ensure tasks are performed the same way every time
- **Quality Assurance**: Maintain standards and reduce errors
- **Training**: Facilitate onboarding and skill development
- **Compliance**: Meet regulatory and audit requirements
- **Knowledge Preservation**: Capture organizational expertise
- **Business Continuity**: Enable operations during staff changes
- **Risk Management**: Minimize errors and safety issues
- **Efficiency**: Streamline operations and reduce redundancy

**Secondary Benefits**:
- Improved accountability through clear role definitions
- Better communication across teams and departments
- Reduced training time and costs
- Enhanced troubleshooting and problem resolution
- Support for continuous improvement initiatives
- Foundation for process automation
- Legal protection and liability reduction

### 2.3 SOP Formatting Standards (Professional)

#### Numbering System:
- **Hierarchical Decimal**: 1.0, 1.1, 1.1.1, 1.1.2, 1.2, 2.0
- **Procedures**: Use numbered lists (1, 2, 3...)
- **Sub-procedures**: Use lettered lists (a, b, c...)
- **Notes/Tips**: Use bullet points (•)
- **Warnings**: Use distinctive markers (⚠️ WARNING:)
- **Maximum Depth**: 4 levels (1.1.1.1)

#### Typography Standards:
- **Headings**: Bold, Title Case (e.g., "1.0 Purpose")
- **Body Text**: 11-12pt, Regular weight, left-aligned
- **Code/System Text**: `Monospace font` (e.g., `ServiceNow`)
- **Emphasis**: **Bold** for critical information
- **Notes**: *Italic* for supplementary information
- **Links**: [Descriptive text](URL) in blue, underlined
- **UI Elements**: Bold for buttons, menus ("Click **Submit**")

#### Page Layout:
- **Margins**: 1 inch (2.54 cm) all sides
- **Line Spacing**: 1.15-1.5 for body text
- **Paragraph Spacing**: 6pt before/after paragraphs
- **Header**: Document title, version, page number (right-aligned)
- **Footer**: Confidentiality statement (left), effective date (center), page X of Y (right)
- **Page Breaks**: Before major sections
- **Widows/Orphans**: Prevent single lines at page breaks

#### Color Coding (When Applicable):
- **Warnings**: Red or orange
- **Notes**: Blue
- **Success**: Green
- **Tips**: Yellow/Gold
- **Links**: Blue (standard web color)

### 3.2 draw.io File Format (.drawio XML)

The .drawio format is an XML-based file format built on the mxGraph library data model.

**File Extension**: `.drawio` (also accepts `.xml`)

**XML Hierarchy**:
```xml
<mxfile host="app.diagrams.net" modified="2026-01-15T10:30:00.000Z"
        agent="REEMA" version="24.0.0" type="device">
  <diagram id="page1" name="Phase 1 - Logging">
    <mxGraphModel dx="1422" dy="762" grid="1" gridSize="10"
                  guides="1" tooltips="1" connect="1" arrows="1"
                  fold="1" page="1" pageScale="1"
                  pageWidth="1169" pageHeight="827" math="0" shadow="0">
      <root>
        <mxCell id="0"/>                          <!-- Root cell (required) -->
        <mxCell id="1" parent="0"/>               <!-- Default layer (required) -->

        <!-- Shapes and connectors go here as child mxCell elements -->

        <mxCell id="2" value="Start" style="ellipse;whiteSpace=wrap;html=1;
                fillColor=#28A745;fontColor=#FFFFFF;strokeColor=#1B7D32;
                fontSize=12;fontStyle=1;"
                vertex="1" parent="1">
          <mxGeometry x="300" y="40" width="120" height="40" as="geometry"/>
        </mxCell>

        <mxCell id="3" value="Receive Incident Report" style="rounded=0;
                whiteSpace=wrap;html=1;fillColor=#1565C0;fontColor=#FFFFFF;
                strokeColor=#0D47A1;fontSize=11;"
                vertex="1" parent="1">
          <mxGeometry x="280" y="120" width="160" height="60" as="geometry"/>
        </mxCell>

        <!-- Edge (connector) between shapes -->
        <mxCell id="e1" style="endArrow=block;endFill=1;html=1;
                strokeColor=#003366;strokeWidth=2;"
                edge="1" source="2" target="3" parent="1">
          <mxGeometry relative="1" as="geometry"/>
        </mxCell>
      </root>
    </mxGraphModel>
  </diagram>
  <!-- Additional pages as separate <diagram> elements -->
  <diagram id="page2" name="Phase 2 - Response">
    <!-- ... -->
  </diagram>
</mxfile>
```

**Key XML Elements**:

| Element | Purpose | Key Attributes |
|---------|---------|----------------|
| `<mxfile>` | Root container | host, modified, agent, version, type |
| `<diagram>` | Single page | id, name (page title) |
| `<mxGraphModel>` | Canvas settings | dx, dy, grid, gridSize, pageWidth, pageHeight |
| `<root>` | Contains all cells | (none) |
| `<mxCell>` | Shape or connector | id, value, style, vertex, edge, source, target, parent |
| `<mxGeometry>` | Position and size | x, y, width, height, relative, as="geometry" |
| `<mxPoint>` | Waypoint or offset | x, y, as="sourcePoint/targetPoint/offset" |

**mxCell Attributes Explained**:
- `id`: Unique identifier (string or number, must be unique per diagram)
- `value`: Text label displayed in the shape (supports HTML when `html=1` in style)
- `style`: Semicolon-delimited style string (see Section 4.4)
- `vertex="1"`: This cell is a shape (not an edge)
- `edge="1"`: This cell is a connector/edge
- `source`: ID of the source shape (for edges)
- `target`: ID of the target shape (for edges)
- `parent`: ID of the parent cell (use "1" for default layer, or container shape ID for swimlanes)
- `connectable="0"`: Used for labels on edges that should not accept connections

**mxGeometry Attributes**:
- `x`: Horizontal position in pixels from left edge of canvas
- `y`: Vertical position in pixels from top edge of canvas
- `width`: Shape width in pixels
- `height`: Shape height in pixels
- `relative="1"`: For edges, indicates relative positioning (0.0-1.0 along edge path)
- `as="geometry"`: Required identifier for mxGeometry elements

**Compressed vs Uncompressed XML**:
- **Uncompressed** (recommended for programmatic generation): Plain XML text, human-readable
- **Compressed** (default in draw.io UI): Raw deflate + Base64 + URL encoding
- To save uncompressed: Edit → Preferences → uncheck "Compressed XML"
- For automation: Always generate uncompressed XML for readability and debugging

**Multi-Page Support**:
- Each `<diagram>` element within `<mxfile>` represents one page
- Pages have unique `id` and descriptive `name` attributes
- Use separate pages for each process phase (Phase 1, Phase 2, etc.)
- Maximum recommended: 8 pages per file

### 3.5 Flowchart Design Principles

**Shape Sizing Standards (Pixels)**:

| Shape Type | Width (px) | Height (px) | Recommended Spacing |
|------------|-----------|-------------|---------------------|
| Terminator (Start/End) | 120 | 40 | 40px below |
| Process Step | 160 | 60 | 40px below |
| Decision Diamond | 200 | 100 | 40px below, 80px to sides |
| Document | 160 | 60 | 40px below |
| Database (Cylinder) | 120 | 80 | 40px below |
| Subprocess | 160 | 60 | 40px below |
| Connector (Off-page) | 40 | 40 | 20px around |

**Layout Guidelines**:
- **Flow Direction**: Top-to-bottom (primary) OR left-to-right (alternative, for wide processes)
- **Never mix flow directions** within the same page
- **Vertical Spacing**: 40px between shapes (minimum 30px)
- **Horizontal Spacing**: 60px between parallel branches (minimum 40px)
- **Center Alignment**: All shapes on the main flow path must be center-aligned (same x-coordinate)
- **Decision Branches**: YES path goes DOWN (main flow), NO path goes RIGHT (exception)
- **Grid Snapping**: Use gridSize=10 in mxGraphModel for clean alignment
- **Maximum Shapes**: 12-15 shapes per page. If more, break into sub-processes on separate pages
- **Canvas Size**: pageWidth=1169, pageHeight=827 (A4 landscape) or pageWidth=827, pageHeight=1169 (A4 portrait)

**Color Coding Standards (House Palette Compliant)**:

|---------|-----------|----------|----------|------------|------------|-------|

### 3.6 Decision Point Formatting

**Decision Diamond Style String**:

- Shape: `rhombus` (200x100 px minimum)
- Decision ID prefix: D1, D2, D3... (bold, first line)

### 3.8 Swimlane Diagrams

Swimlane diagrams show responsibility areas (who does what) and are essential for RACI-aligned process documentation.

**Swimlane Architecture in draw.io**:
- **Pool**: Top-level container (the entire process)
- **Lanes**: Divisions within the pool (one per role/team)
- Shapes are placed INSIDE lanes via the `parent` attribute
- Lanes are children of the pool via `parent` attribute

**Horizontal Swimlane XML Structure**:

- Use 3-5 lanes maximum per diagram for readability

- Each lane header should match a RACI role
- Cross-lane connectors should be clear and labeled
- Use the pool `parent` for edges that cross lanes
- Maintain consistent lane heights (120-150px per lane)

### 3.9 Layers for Complex Diagrams

Layers allow you to create multi-dimensional flowcharts with stakeholder-specific views.

**Layer XML Structure**:
```xml
<root>
  <mxCell id="0"/>                                    <!-- Root -->
  <mxCell id="1" parent="0"/>                         <!-- Default Layer (always visible) -->
  <mxCell id="layer_detail" value="Detailed Steps" parent="0" visible="1"/>   <!-- Detail Layer -->
```

Shapes are assigned to layers via their `parent` attribute:
```xml
<!-- Shape on default layer -->
<mxCell id="s1" value="Main Process" parent="1" vertex="1" .../>

<!-- Shape on detail layer -->
<mxCell id="s_detail" value="Sub-step 2.1" parent="layer_detail" vertex="1" .../>

<!-- Shape on systems layer -->
<mxCell id="s_system" value="ServiceNow API" parent="layer_systems" vertex="1" .../>
```

**Recommended Layer Strategy for IT SOPs**:

### 3.12 Raw XML Generation from Python

For maximum control over flowchart generation, generate .drawio XML directly using Python's built-in `xml.etree.ElementTree`.

**Complete Flowchart Generator Module**:
```python
"""draw.io XML Generator for REEMA SOP Flowcharts."""

import xml.etree.ElementTree as ET
from typing import List, Dict, Optional, Tuple


class DrawioFlowchartGenerator:
    """Generates .drawio XML files for IT process flowcharts."""

    # House Palette Color Palette
    COLORS = {
        "start": {"fill": "#28A745", "stroke": "#1B7D32", "font": "#FFFFFF"},
        "end": {"fill": "#DC3545", "stroke": "#A71D2A", "font": "#FFFFFF"},
        "phase1": {"fill": "#1565C0", "stroke": "#0D47A1", "font": "#FFFFFF"},
        "phase2": {"fill": "#E65100", "stroke": "#BF360C", "font": "#FFFFFF"},
        "phase3": {"fill": "#2E7D32", "stroke": "#1B5E20", "font": "#FFFFFF"},
        "phase4": {"fill": "#6A1B9A", "stroke": "#4A148C", "font": "#FFFFFF"},
        "decision": {"fill": "#FFC107", "stroke": "#B28704", "font": "#000000"},
        "navy": {"fill": "#1F3A5F", "stroke": "#142840", "font": "#FFFFFF"},
        "amber": {"fill": "#FFC107", "stroke": "#B28704", "font": "#000000"},
        "exception": {"fill": "#FFCDD2", "stroke": "#E57373", "font": "#B71C1C"},
        "approval": {"fill": "#00695C", "stroke": "#004D40", "font": "#FFFFFF"},
    }

    CONNECTOR_COLOR = "#003366"

    def __init__(self):
        self._id_counter = 2  # 0 and 1 are reserved
        self._shapes = []
        self._edges = []

    def _next_id(self) -> str:
        """Generate unique cell ID."""
        self._id_counter += 1
        return str(self._id_counter)

    def create_file(self, pages: List[Dict]) -> str:
        """Create complete .drawio XML file.

        Args:
            pages: List of page dicts with 'name' and 'shapes' keys

        Returns:
            Complete .drawio XML string
        """
        mxfile = ET.Element("mxfile", {
            "host": "app.diagrams.net",
            "agent": "REEMA-Saad",
            "version": "24.0.0",
            "type": "device",
        })

        for page_data in pages:
            self._id_counter = 2
            diagram = ET.SubElement(mxfile, "diagram", {
                "id": f"page_{page_data['name'].replace(' ', '_')}",
                "name": page_data["name"],
            })

            model = ET.SubElement(diagram, "mxGraphModel", {
                "dx": "1422", "dy": "762", "grid": "1", "gridSize": "10",
                "guides": "1", "tooltips": "1", "connect": "1", "arrows": "1",
                "fold": "1", "page": "1", "pageScale": "1",
                "pageWidth": "1169", "pageHeight": "827",
                "math": "0", "shadow": "0",
            })

            root = ET.SubElement(model, "root")
            ET.SubElement(root, "mxCell", {"id": "0"})
            ET.SubElement(root, "mxCell", {"id": "1", "parent": "0"})

            # Build shapes and edges from page_data
            if "elements" in page_data:
                self._build_elements(root, page_data["elements"])

        return ET.tostring(mxfile, encoding="unicode", xml_declaration=True)

    def add_shape(self, root: ET.Element, value: str, x: int, y: int,
                  width: int = 160, height: int = 60,
                  shape_type: str = "process", phase: str = "phase1") -> str:
        """Add a shape to the diagram.

        Args:
            root: XML root element
            value: Shape label text
            x, y: Position in pixels
            width, height: Dimensions
            shape_type: One of 'start', 'end', 'process', 'decision', 'document', 'database'
            phase: Color phase key

        Returns:
            Shape ID
        """
        cell_id = self._next_id()
        colors = self.COLORS.get(phase, self.COLORS["phase1"])

        style_parts = []
        if shape_type == "start" or shape_type == "end":
            style_parts.append("ellipse")
            colors = self.COLORS[shape_type]
            width, height = 120, 40
        elif shape_type == "decision":
            style_parts.append("rhombus")
            colors = self.COLORS["decision"]
            width, height = 200, 100
        elif shape_type == "document":
            style_parts.append("shape=document")
        elif shape_type == "database":
            style_parts.append("shape=cylinder3;boundedLbl=1;size=15")
        else:
            style_parts.append("rounded=0")

        style_parts.extend([
            "whiteSpace=wrap", "html=1",
            f"fillColor={colors['fill']}",
            f"fontColor={colors['font']}",
            f"strokeColor={colors['stroke']}",
            f"fontSize={'12' if shape_type in ('start', 'end') else '11'}",
            f"fontStyle={'1' if shape_type in ('start', 'end', 'decision') else '0'}",
            f"strokeWidth={'2' if shape_type in ('start', 'end', 'decision') else '1'}",
        ])

        style = ";".join(style_parts) + ";"

        cell = ET.SubElement(root, "mxCell", {
            "id": cell_id, "value": value, "style": style,
            "vertex": "1", "parent": "1",
        })
        ET.SubElement(cell, "mxGeometry", {
            "x": str(x), "y": str(y),
            "width": str(width), "height": str(height),
            "as": "geometry",
        })
        return cell_id

    def add_edge(self, root: ET.Element, source_id: str, target_id: str,
                 label: str = "", edge_type: str = "normal") -> str:
        """Add a connector edge between two shapes.

        Args:
            root: XML root element
            source_id: Source shape ID
            target_id: Target shape ID
            label: Edge label text
            edge_type: One of 'normal', 'yes', 'no', 'dashed', 'escalation'

        Returns:
            Edge ID
        """
        cell_id = self._next_id()

        style_map = {
            "normal": f"endArrow=block;endFill=1;html=1;strokeColor={self.CONNECTOR_COLOR};strokeWidth=2;",
            "yes": "endArrow=block;endFill=1;html=1;strokeColor=#28A745;strokeWidth=2;fontColor=#28A745;fontSize=10;fontStyle=1;labelBackgroundColor=#FFFFFF;",
            "no": "endArrow=block;endFill=1;html=1;strokeColor=#DC3545;strokeWidth=2;fontColor=#DC3545;fontSize=10;fontStyle=1;labelBackgroundColor=#FFFFFF;",
            "dashed": f"endArrow=block;endFill=1;html=1;strokeColor={self.CONNECTOR_COLOR};strokeWidth=1;dashed=1;dashPattern=3 3;",
            "escalation": "endArrow=block;endFill=1;html=1;strokeColor=#E65100;strokeWidth=3;fontColor=#E65100;fontSize=10;fontStyle=1;",
        }

        cell = ET.SubElement(root, "mxCell", {
            "id": cell_id, "value": label, "style": style_map.get(edge_type, style_map["normal"]),
            "edge": "1", "source": source_id, "target": target_id, "parent": "1",
        })
        ET.SubElement(cell, "mxGeometry", {"relative": "1", "as": "geometry"})
        return cell_id

    def _build_elements(self, root: ET.Element, elements: List[Dict]):
        """Build shapes and edges from element definitions."""
        id_map = {}
        for el in elements:
            if el["type"] in ("start", "end", "process", "decision", "document", "database"):
                shape_id = self.add_shape(
                    root, el["value"], el["x"], el["y"],
                    el.get("width", 160), el.get("height", 60),
                    el["type"], el.get("phase", "phase1")
                )
                id_map[el["id"]] = shape_id
            elif el["type"] == "edge":
                self.add_edge(
                    root, id_map[el["source"]], id_map[el["target"]],
                    el.get("label", ""), el.get("edge_type", "normal")
                )


def generate_incident_flowchart() -> str:
```

### 3.14 Multi-Page Diagram Strategy

Complex IT processes should be split across multiple pages for readability.

**Multi-Page XML Structure**:
```xml
<mxfile host="app.diagrams.net" agent="REEMA-Saad">
  <diagram id="phase1" name="Phase 1 - Logging">
    <mxGraphModel ...>
      <root>
        <!-- Phase 1 shapes and edges -->
      </root>
    </mxGraphModel>
  </diagram>
  <diagram id="phase2" name="Phase 2 - Response">
    <mxGraphModel ...>
      <root>
        <!-- Phase 2 shapes and edges -->
      </root>
    </mxGraphModel>
  </diagram>
  <diagram id="phase3" name="Phase 3 - Resolution">
    <!-- ... -->
  </diagram>
  <diagram id="overview" name="Overview - High Level">
    <!-- Simplified overview linking to detail pages -->
  </diagram>
</mxfile>
```

**Page Strategy by Process Complexity**:

| Process | Recommended Pages | Page Names |
|---------|------------------|------------|
| Incident Management | 4-5 | Phase 1-4 + Overview |
| Change Management | 4-5 | RFC, CAB, Implementation, PIR + Overview |
| Problem Management | 3-4 | Detection, RCA, Resolution + Overview |
| Service Request | 2-3 | Request, Fulfillment + Overview |
| Access Management | 2-3 | Provisioning, Review/Revocation + Overview |
| Event Management | 2-3 | Detection, Response + Overview |

**Cross-Page References**:
- Use "Off-page Connector" shapes (`ellipse;aspect=fixed;`) with matching labels
- Label format: "→ See Phase 2" or "From Phase 1 →"
- Use hyperlinks on shapes to link between pages (draw.io supports inter-page linking)

**Naming Convention**:
```
SOP_[ProcessName]_V[X]_Flowchart_[PhaseName].drawio

Examples:
SOP_Incident_Management_V3_Flowchart_AllPhases.drawio
SOP_Change_Management_V2_Flowchart_AllPhases.drawio
SOP_Problem_Management_V1_Flowchart_AllPhases.drawio
```

**Python Subprocess Wrapper**:
```python
import subprocess
import os

def export_drawio(input_path: str, output_path: str,
                  format: str = "png", page_index: int = 0,
                  scale: int = 2, border: int = 10) -> bool:
    """Export .drawio file to image/PDF format.

    Args:
        input_path: Path to .drawio file
        output_path: Output file path
        format: Output format (png, pdf, svg, jpg)
        page_index: Page to export (0-indexed)
        scale: Scale factor (1-4)
        border: Border padding in pixels

    Returns:
        True if export succeeded
    """
    drawio_cmd = "/Applications/draw.io.app/Contents/MacOS/draw.io"

    cmd = [
        drawio_cmd, "--export",
        "--format", format,
        "--output", output_path,
        "--page-index", str(page_index),
        "--scale", str(scale),
        "--border", str(border),
        "--no-sandbox",
        input_path,
    ]

    try:
        result = subprocess.run(cmd, capture_output=True, text=True, timeout=30)
        return result.returncode == 0
    except (subprocess.TimeoutExpired, FileNotFoundError):
        return False
```

### 3.19 Common draw.io Mistakes to Avoid

**Layout Mistakes**:
- Mixing flow directions (top-to-bottom AND left-to-right in same page)
- Inconsistent spacing between shapes (some 40px, some 80px)
- Shapes not aligned to grid (misaligned x-coordinates)
- Too many shapes on one page (>15 shapes, creating visual clutter)
- Decision branches that cross the main flow path

**Shape Mistakes**:
- Using rectangle for decisions (must use `rhombus`)
- Inconsistent shape sizes (process boxes of different widths)
- Missing Start/End terminators (every page needs clear entry/exit points)
- Orphan shapes with no incoming or outgoing connections

**Text Mistakes**:
- Text too small to read (fontSize < 10)
- Missing labels on decision edges (YES/NO not shown)
- Unclear decision questions (not in yes/no format)
- Using `\n` instead of `&#xa;` for line breaks (renders as literal text)
- Abbreviations without definition

**Color Mistakes**:
- Black text (#000000) on dark backgrounds (unreadable)
- Inconsistent color coding (same phase using different colors)
- Too many colors (>6 distinct fill colors creates confusion)
- Colors that lose meaning in black & white printing

**Connector Mistakes**:
- Curved connectors instead of orthogonal (looks unprofessional)
- Missing arrow heads on flow lines (unclear direction)
- Connectors not attached to shapes (floating edges)
- Decision YES/NO paths both going same direction
- Edge labels without white background (hard to read over shapes)

**XML / Technical Mistakes**:
- Duplicate mxCell IDs (causes rendering errors)
- Missing root cells (id=0 and id=1 are required)
- Incorrect `parent` attribute for swimlane children
- Using `source`/`target` on vertex cells (only valid on edges)
- Forgetting `vertex="1"` on shapes or `edge="1"` on connectors

```markdown
## 4.0 DEFINITIONS AND ACRONYMS

### 4.1 Key Terms

**Change**: The addition, modification, or removal of anything that could affect IT services. Changes are managed through the Change Management process.

**Configuration Item (CI)**: Any component that needs to be managed to deliver an IT service. Examples include hardware, software, documentation, and personnel records.

**Escalation**: The transfer of an incident to a higher level of technical expertise (functional escalation) or management authority (hierarchical escalation).

**First Call Resolution (FCR)**: Resolution of an incident during the initial contact with the user, without requiring callbacks or escalation to other teams.

**Impact**: A measure of the effect of an incident on business processes or the number of users affected. Measured as High, Medium, or Low.

**Incident**: An unplanned interruption to an IT service or reduction in quality of an IT service. Examples: email unavailable, application crash, network outage.

**Major Incident**: An incident with highest impact requiring immediate attention and specialized procedures. Typically affects multiple users or critical business services.

**Priority**: A category used to identify the relative importance of an incident based on Impact and Urgency. Determines the order and timeframe for resolution.

**Service Desk**: The single point of contact between the service provider and users for day-to-day incidents and service requests.

**Service Level Agreement (SLA)**: A formal agreement documenting target response times and resolution times for incidents based on priority.

**Urgency**: A measure of how quickly a business process requires resolution. Measured as High, Medium, or Low based on business impact.

**Workaround**: A temporary solution that reduces or eliminates the impact of an incident for which a full resolution is not yet available.
```

```
## 7.0 DETAILED PROCEDURES

### 7.1 Incident Detection and Logging

**Objective**: Create complete and accurate incident record in ServiceNow within 5 minutes of incident report.

**Prerequisites**:
- Active ServiceNow account with Incident Management module access
- Valid Service Desk agent credentials
- Access to user directory and CMDB
- Understanding of incident categorization scheme

**Estimated Time**: 3-5 minutes per incident

**Procedure**:

1. **Receive Incident Notification**
   - Answer phone call, email, chat, or portal submission
   - Greet user professionally: "Thank you for contacting IT Service Desk. My name is [Your Name]. How may I help you?"
   - Expected Result: User begins describing the issue

2. **Open ServiceNow Incident Form**
   - Log into ServiceNow
   - Navigate to **Incident > Create New**
   - Alternative: Use keyboard shortcut (Ctrl+Alt+I)
   - Expected Result: Blank incident form displays

3. **Record Caller Information**
   a. Click in the **Caller** field
   b. Begin typing user's name or employee ID
   c. Select correct user from auto-complete list
   d. Verify phone number and email auto-populate
   e. If user not found, click **Create New User** (rare)
   - Expected Result: Caller information section completely filled

4. **Document Short Description**
   - Click in **Short Description** field
   - Use format: "[Service/System] - [Brief Issue Summary]"
   - Examples:
     - "Email - Cannot send messages with attachments"
     - "VPN - Connection fails with error 800"
     - "Laptop - Blue screen on startup"
   - Keep to 100 characters or less
   - Expected Result: Clear, searchable summary created

5. **Capture Detailed Description**
   - Click in **Description** field
   - Document the following information:
     a. What is not working? (Symptoms)
     b. When did it start? (Timeframe)
     c. What error messages appear? (Exact text)
     d. What steps reproduce the issue? (Actions taken)
     e. How many users affected? (Scope)
     f. What is the business impact? (Urgency)
   - Use bullet points for clarity
   - Include screenshots if user can email them
   - Expected Result: Comprehensive technical context captured

6. **Categorize the Incident**
   - Click **Category** dropdown
   - Select appropriate first-level category:
     - Hardware (Desktops, Laptops, Printers, Phones)
     - Software (Applications, Operating System, Email)
     - Network (Connectivity, VPN, WiFi)
     - Access (Password Reset, Account Unlock, Permissions)
   - Select **Subcategory** (e.g., Software > Email > Cannot Send)
   - Expected Result: Category determines auto-assignment rules

7. **Assess Impact**
   - Click **Impact** dropdown
   - Select impact level:
     - **1 - High**: Multiple users OR critical business service affected
     - **2 - Medium**: Single user with significant business impact OR VIP user
     - **3 - Low**: Single user with minimal business impact
   - Consider: Number of affected users, criticality of service, business process impact
   - Expected Result: Impact documented

8. **Assess Urgency**
   - Click **Urgency** dropdown
   - Select urgency level:
     - **1 - High**: Work completely stopped, immediate business impact
     - **2 - Medium**: Work significantly slowed, workaround unavailable
     - **3 - Low**: Minor inconvenience, workaround available
   - Ask user: "On a scale of 1-3, how urgently do you need this resolved?"
   - Expected Result: Urgency documented

9. **Review Auto-Calculated Priority**
   - System automatically calculates **Priority** from Impact and Urgency:

     | Impact/Urgency | High (1) | Medium (2) | Low (3) |
     |----------------|----------|------------|---------|
     | High (1)       | P1       | P2         | P3      |
     | Medium (2)     | P2       | P3         | P4      |
     | Low (3)        | P3       | P4         | P5      |

   - Verify priority is appropriate
   - Override only if justified (requires supervisor approval)
   - Expected Result: Priority drives SLA clock

10. **Add Contact Preference**
    - In **Contact Type** field, select user's preferred communication method
    - Options: Email, Phone, Teams Chat, No Contact Required
    - Record best phone number and email in notes
    - Expected Result: Agent knows how to reach user

11. **Link Configuration Item (CI)**
    - Click **Configuration Item** lookup icon
    - Search for affected hardware/software in CMDB
    - Select appropriate CI (e.g., user's laptop, email server)
    - If not found, leave blank (will be added later)
    - Expected Result: Incident linked to affected asset

12. **Submit the Incident**
    - Review all fields for completeness and accuracy
    - Click **Submit** button (bottom right)
    - Expected Result:
      - Incident number generated (format: INC0001234)
      - Incident appears in queue
      - SLA clock starts
      - Auto-notification sent to caller

13. **Communicate Incident Number to User**
    - Read incident number clearly: "Your incident number is INC0001234"
    - Spell out if needed: "That's India November Charlie, zero zero zero one two three four"
    - Explain: "You can use this number to check status or call back for updates"
    - Expected Result: User has reference number

14. **Set Expectations**
    - Based on priority, communicate SLA target:
      - P1: "We'll respond within 15 minutes and work to resolve within 4 hours"
      - P2: "We'll respond within 1 hour and work to resolve within 8 hours"
      - P3: "We'll respond within 4 hours and work to resolve within 24 hours"
      - P4: "We'll respond within 8 hours and work to resolve within 48 hours"
      - P5: "We'll respond within 24 hours and work to resolve within 5 business days"
    - Expected Result: User understands timeline

15. **Provide Next Steps**
    - If you will troubleshoot immediately: "Let me work on this now and I'll have an update for you shortly"
    - If assigning to another team: "I'm routing this to our [Team Name] who specializes in these issues. They will contact you shortly"
    - Expected Result: User knows what to expect

**Post-Procedure Actions**:
- Incident appears in appropriate queue (based on assignment rules)
- SLA timer displays on incident (e.g., "Response Due: 15 minutes")
- Automated email sent to user with incident number and summary
- If P1 Critical: Automatic notification to Major Incident Manager

**Verification Checklist**:
- [ ] Incident number generated
- [ ] All required fields completed
- [ ] Description is clear and detailed
- [ ] Category and priority are accurate
- [ ] Configuration Item linked (if known)
- [ ] User has incident number
- [ ] Expectations set appropriately

**Troubleshooting**:
- **User Not Found in System**: Click "Create New User", enter name, email, department, manager
- **Category Unclear**: Consult Category Guide (Appendix B) or ask Team Lead
- **Priority Override Needed**: Obtain supervisor approval before changing calculated priority
- **System Slow/Unresponsive**: Log incident in backup system (Excel tracker), enter in ServiceNow when available
- **User Unable to Describe Issue**: Ask guided questions: "What were you trying to do? What happened instead? When did this start?"

### 7.6 Major Incident Management

**Objective**: Coordinate rapid response to critical incidents affecting multiple users or critical business services.

**Prerequisites**:
- Major Incident Manager on-call (24/7 availability)
- Major Incident Bridge Line configured
- Emergency contact list updated
- Crisis communication templates ready

**Major Incident Criteria**:
An incident is classified as "Major" if it meets ANY of the following:
- Impact: Affects 50+ users OR entire business unit
- Criticality: Critical business service completely unavailable
- Duration: Significant service degradation lasting 2+ hours
- Financial: Potential financial loss exceeds $50,000
- Regulatory: Compliance or regulatory implications
- Reputation: High risk of reputational damage
- Executive: Affects C-level executives or Board members
- Customer-Facing: External customer services impacted

**Major Incident Procedure**:

1. **Declare Major Incident**
   - Service Desk Agent or IT Manager declares major incident
   - Update incident priority to P1 Critical
   - Check "Major Incident" flag in ServiceNow
   - Expected Result: Major Incident Manager paged automatically

2. **Assemble Major Incident Team**
   - Major Incident Manager calls emergency bridge line
   - Page required SMEs based on affected systems:
     - Network Team (if network issue)
     - Application Team (if application issue)
     - Database Team (if database issue)
     - Security Team (if security concern)
     - Vendor Support (if third-party system)
   - Page IT Management and Business Stakeholders
   - Expected Result: Team assembled within 15 minutes

3. **Establish Command and Control**
   - Major Incident Manager chairs bridge call
   - Assign scribe to document timeline
   - Assign technical coordinator to lead investigation
   - Assign communications coordinator for stakeholder updates
   - Expected Result: Clear roles and leadership

4. **Assess Situation**
   - Determine scope of impact (users, systems, locations)
   - Identify affected business processes
   - Estimate financial impact
   - Review monitoring data and logs
   - Expected Result: Situation clearly understood

5. **Implement Immediate Actions**
   - Mobilize technical teams for diagnosis
   - Implement immediate workarounds if available
   - Invoke disaster recovery procedures if needed
   - Isolate affected systems if security incident
   - Expected Result: Steps taken to limit damage

6. **Communicate Stakeholders**
   - Send initial communication within 30 minutes:
     - What is affected
     - How many users impacted
     - Current status
     - Next update time
   - Distribution: IT Management, Business Unit Heads, Affected Users
   - Use templates from Crisis Communication Playbook
   - Expected Result: Stakeholders informed

7. **Conduct Investigation**
   - Technical teams investigate root cause in parallel
   - Share findings on bridge call every 30 minutes
   - Test theories systematically
   - Engage vendors if needed
   - Expected Result: Root cause identified

8. **Implement Resolution**
   - Develop resolution plan
   - Assess risks of implementation
   - Obtain approvals for emergency changes
   - Implement fix in production
   - Expected Result: Service restored

9. **Verify Service Restoration**
   - Test affected systems thoroughly
   - Verify users can work normally
   - Monitor for stability (30-60 minutes)
   - Expected Result: Service confirmed operational

10. **Communicate Resolution**
    - Send resolution communication to all stakeholders
    - Include: What was fixed, when service restored, any follow-up actions
    - Thank teams for response
    - Expected Result: Stakeholders notified

11. **Close Bridge Call**
    - Major Incident Manager officially closes incident
    - Thank all participants
    - Schedule Post-Incident Review (within 48 hours)
    - Expected Result: Bridge call ended

12. **Document Timeline**
    - Update incident with complete timeline
    - Include all actions, decisions, and communications
    - Attach relevant logs, screenshots, and data
    - Expected Result: Complete audit trail

**Post-Major-Incident Activities**:

13. **Conduct Post-Incident Review (PIR)**
    - Schedule within 48 hours
    - Attendees: All major incident team members
    - Review timeline and response
    - Identify what went well and areas for improvement
    - Document lessons learned
    - Create action items for improvements
    - Expected Result: PIR report completed

14. **Implement Improvements**
    - Assign owners for improvement actions
    - Update SOPs, runbooks, and documentation
    - Improve monitoring and alerting if gaps identified
    - Provide additional training if needed
    - Expected Result: Organization learns from incident

**Major Incident Communication Template**:

```
SUBJECT: [MAJOR INCIDENT] [System Name] - [Brief Description]

SEVERITY: P1 Critical
INCIDENT #: INC0001234
STATUS: [Investigating / Identified / Resolving / Resolved]

[Time estimate or "Under investigation"]

```
## 8.0 ESCALATION PROCEDURES

### 8.1 Functional Escalation (Technical)

**When to Escalate**:
- Issue exceeds Level 1 troubleshooting capabilities
- Specialized expertise required
- Multiple troubleshooting attempts failed
- User requests escalation
- Incident unresolved after 30 minutes (P1/P2) or 2 hours (P3/P4)

**Escalation Path**:
Service Desk (L1) → Technical Support (L2) → Senior Engineers (L3) → Subject Matter Experts → Vendor Support

**Procedure**:
1. Document all troubleshooting steps taken in work notes
2. Summarize issue clearly for receiving team
3. Update **Assignment Group** field to target team
4. Set **State** to "Awaiting Assignment"
5. Add **Escalation** work note explaining reason
6. Call or Teams message receiving team for immediate handoff
7. Notify user of escalation

### 8.2 Hierarchical Escalation (Management)

**When to Escalate**:
- SLA breach imminent or occurred
- User is dissatisfied and requesting manager
- Policy exception needed
- Resource allocation required
- Incident requires management decision
- Major incident declaration needed

**Escalation Hierarchy**:
Service Desk Agent → Service Desk Team Lead → IT Operations Manager → IT Director → CIO

**Procedure**:
1. Attempt to resolve at current level first
2. Brief manager on situation, actions taken, and required decision
3. Update incident with escalation notes
4. Manager reviews and takes action or escalates further
5. Document decision and next steps

### 8.3 Vendor Escalation

**When to Escalate**:
- Issue identified with third-party product
- Internal troubleshooting exhausted
- Vendor-specific knowledge required
- Software bug suspected
- Hardware failure confirmed

**Procedure**:
1. Check vendor support contract and entitlements
2. Gather diagnostic data (logs, error messages, screenshots)
3. Open vendor support ticket via appropriate channel
4. Link vendor ticket number to ServiceNow incident
5. Monitor vendor progress and update internal incident
6. Test vendor-provided solution before implementing in production

### 8.5 SLA-Based Automatic Escalation

ServiceNow automatically escalates incidents when:

- Any: SLA breach occurs → Notify IT Operations Manager immediately
- Any: Second SLA breach in 30 days → Trigger process review

### APPENDIX A: Incident Priority Matrix

| Impact → Urgency ↓ | High Impact (1) | Medium Impact (2) | Low Impact (3) |
|--------------------|-----------------|-------------------|----------------|
| **High Urgency (1)** | **P1 Critical** | **P2 High** | P3 Medium |
| **Medium Urgency (2)** | **P2 High** | P3 Medium | P4 Low |
| **Low Urgency (3)** | P3 Medium | P4 Low | P5 Planning |

**Priority Descriptions**:

**P1 - Critical**:
- Multiple users unable to work OR critical business service down
- Immediate business impact, financial loss occurring
- No workaround available
- SLA: 15-minute response, 4-hour resolution
- Example: Email system down for entire company

**P2 - High**:
- Significant business impact, but not critical
- Workaround may be available but inconvenient
- Affects important business processes
- SLA: 1-hour response, 8-hour resolution
- Example: VPN issues affecting remote workers

**P3 - Medium**:
- Moderate business impact
- Workaround available
- Single user or small group affected
- SLA: 4-hour response, 24-hour resolution
- Example: Printer not working for one user

**P4 - Low**:
- Minimal business impact
- User can continue working
- Non-urgent issue
- SLA: 8-hour response, 48-hour resolution
- Example: Minor software question

**P5 - Planning**:
- No immediate business impact
- Request for information or future planning
- SLA: 24-hour response, 5 business days resolution
- Example: Request for software evaluation

### APPENDIX C: Troubleshooting Quick Reference

**Email Issues**:
1. Check internet connectivity
2. Verify email client settings (IMAP/SMTP)
3. Test webmail access
4. Check mailbox quota
5. Review Outlook .OST file integrity
6. Test on different device/network

**Network Connectivity Issues**:
1. Check physical connections (cables, WiFi)
2. Run ipconfig /release and ipconfig /renew
3. Flush DNS cache (ipconfig /flushdns)
4. Ping gateway (ping 192.168.1.1)
5. Ping external IP (ping 8.8.8.8)
6. Ping external hostname (ping google.com)
7. Check firewall settings
8. Test on different network

**VPN Connection Issues**:
1. Verify VPN client version (update if needed)
2. Check internet connectivity before connecting
3. Verify credentials (username/password)
4. Try different VPN server/gateway
5. Disable antivirus temporarily
6. Check for split-tunnel configuration
7. Review VPN logs for error codes

**Password Reset**:
1. Verify user identity (employee ID, manager name, DOB)
2. Check if account is locked or expired
3. Reset in Active Directory Users and Computers
4. Unlock if necessary
5. Force password change at next logon (if required)
6. Verify user can login to workstation
7. Check email connectivity after password change

**Software Installation Issues**:
1. Verify user has admin rights (or use elevated prompt)
2. Check system requirements (OS version, RAM, disk space)
3. Disable antivirus temporarily
4. Download fresh installer (corrupted file?)
5. Check for conflicting software
6. Review installation logs
7. Try compatibility mode (if legacy software)
```

```
### APPENDIX F: Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

**Q: What's the difference between an incident and a service request?**
A: An incident is an unplanned interruption or reduction in service quality. A service request is a formal request for something new or a standard change (e.g., software installation, access request). If something is broken or not working, it's an incident. If you're requesting something new, it's a service request.

**Q: Why do you need so much information when I log an incident?**
A: Detailed information helps us resolve your issue faster. The more context we have (symptoms, error messages, when it started), the quicker we can diagnose and fix the problem. Complete information often means we can resolve your issue on the first call.

**Q: Can I request a specific technician?**
A: While we appreciate that you may have worked well with a specific technician, incidents are assigned based on workload, expertise, and availability. Our entire team is trained to provide excellent support. If you have a complex ongoing issue, we can coordinate to have the same person follow up.

**Q: Why was my incident closed? The problem still exists!**
A: We apologize if your issue wasn't fully resolved. Please reply to the closure email or call us back with your incident number, and we'll reopen the ticket immediately. We only close incidents after user confirmation, so this may have been miscommunication.

**Q: How do I check the status of my incident?**
A: You can check status in several ways:
1. Self-service portal: https://company.service-now.com
2. Email: All updates are sent to your email automatically
3. Phone: Call Service Desk with your incident number
4. Teams: Use the ServiceNow bot to check status

**Q: What if my incident isn't resolved within the SLA time?**
A: If we can't resolve your incident within the SLA, your Service Desk Team Lead and IT Manager are automatically notified. They will contact you to provide an update and ensure all possible resources are engaged. Major incidents have escalation procedures to involve senior management if needed.

**Q: Can I submit an incident for someone else?**
A: Yes, you can report an incident on behalf of another user (e.g., if they're unable to access their computer). Please provide their name, employee ID, and contact information so we can follow up directly with them for troubleshooting.

**Q: What happens after hours and on weekends?**
A: Our Service Desk operates 24/7 for critical issues. P1 and P2 incidents are handled around the clock. P3-P5 incidents logged after hours will be addressed during business hours unless they are escalated to critical.

**Q: How do I escalate an issue?**
A: You can request escalation at any time by:
1. Asking the agent you're working with
2. Calling and asking for a supervisor
3. Replying to incident emails requesting escalation
4. Contacting IT Management directly (contact info in Section 8.4)

**Q: Will my data be safe when a technician remotes into my computer?**
A: Yes, we follow strict security protocols. Technicians only access systems with your permission and only view information necessary to resolve the incident. All remote sessions are logged and audited. Never share passwords—we'll never ask for them.
```
